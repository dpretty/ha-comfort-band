# Comfort Band

A Home Assistant custom integration for per-room HVAC band-control.

## What it does

Most thermostats hold a single setpoint, which makes heat pumps and mini-splits short-cycle as the room temperature drifts above and below the target. Comfort Band lets you define a **low/high band per room**: the system heats when the room falls below the low, cools when it climbs above the high, and stays idle (`fan_only`) in between.

Each zone gets its own band, override, and per-profile schedule. A **profile** (e.g. `home`, `away`) lets you swap every zone's schedule with a single tap or service call.

## Status

**v0.22.1** makes a flat run's slope exactly flat. A slope is a weighted least-squares fit over the latest run of an action, and when every reading in the run is the same that fit is 0. In floating point it came out as the rounding left between two equal products instead: about ±1e-15 °C/min, with its sign set by the weights, which move with the clock. The v0.15.0 sign guard compares a recovery slope against exactly zero. So a flat heat or cool run was discarded at one refresh and kept at the next, and since v0.21.0 a flat previous cycle could lend its rounding to a young one.

**Why that matters.** Kept, the rounding has MPC plan heating as holding the room where it is. Below the band, an hour of that scores no time in band, and nor does an hour of slow upward idle drift, but idling ends nearer the band, so MPC idles there. In a test zone driven through the real listeners, with the room held 0.35 °C under a 19.2–21.0 °C band by a reading that doesn't move, MPC released the heating three times in an hour and a half and idled for a third of the refreshes; each time the rounding came out negative, the reactive path heated again. v0.22.0's hold already keeps a young cycle heating on a borrowed flat slope. It doesn't cover a cycle whose own flat run has grown old enough to stand, or an idle zone below its band after a flat heat cycle.

**Who sees it.** A run of one reading needs refreshes that leave the reading alone. Most often they come from:
- a zone whose humidity sensor updates with **Use apparent temperature** off, since the update still triggers a refresh;
- a temperature sensor with `force_update`, which reports unchanged values.

With apparent temperature on, temperature and humidity would both have to repeat. In ten days of five such zones' history no heat or cool run was flat.

**The fix.** The fit is now taken relative to the run's first reading. That leaves every slope as it was, but a run of one reading fits exactly 0, and the sign guard discards it at every refresh: `sensor.{zone}_thermal_slope` shows `recovery_slope_*` as `None` with `method_recovery_* = "rejected"`. Before, it flickered between that and `wls` with a slope of 0.0. A discarded slope can't be lent either, so MPC has no slope for that action and the reactive path heats or cools as it would without MPC. An idle run of one reading reads exactly 0 too.

**Why not a flatness threshold.** The obvious alternative was to treat any recovery slope under the predictor's 0.05 °C/h flatness threshold as flat. Replayed over ten days of five zones' history (13,746 refreshes), it had no flat run to discard. It did discard 20 real fits from 14 runs that had moved the room 0.2–1.8 °C: long cycles whose latest samples had levelled off, or caught a unit cycling its compressor. That took MPC's readiness away at 13 refreshes and changed 3 decisions. Twice it released a cool cycle MPC was keeping on at the top of the band. Once it idled a room 0.56 °C below its band for a refresh where v0.22.0 heated at once. The smallest recovery fit in that history was 0.005 °C/h; the rounding is under 1e-12 °C/h.

**Measured.**
- Replay: the same ten days of five zones changed no decision, readiness or `method_*` at any refresh. The largest change to any fit was 3e-13 °C/h.
- Synthetic flat runs: 1,600 of them, 4–8 samples each. v0.22.0 fitted 504 negative, 460 positive and 636 exactly zero, and 1,597 changed sign within ten refreshes. This release fits all 1,600 exactly 0 at every refresh.

**v0.22.0** gives MPC switching hysteresis. MPC scores constant actions over its horizon by time in band, with nothing charged for switching, so around the room temperature at which running and idling score the same, it switches bang-bang. In ten days of five zones' recorded history it ended 104 cycles itself and restarted the same action within 15 minutes after 81 of them, a median 4.9 minutes later. Since v0.21.0 MPC keeps the cycles it starts, and a cycle started at that temperature has usually taken the room back across it a refresh or two later, so MPC ended it there, a sample or two in.

**Why that costs more than a wasted start.** A cycle that young is planned on the previous cycle's slope (v0.21.0), and a run of a sample or two is too short to fit, so it has nothing to lend. The next cycle MPC starts finds nothing to borrow, so MPC is no longer ready and the reactive path takes the room until a cycle of its own runs long enough to fit. A reactive path that ends its cycles early may never get there. A new event-driven test runs one room through both versions. On v0.21.0, MPC cools for one refresh, starts again ten minutes later, finds at the next refresh that the one-sample run before it has nothing to lend, and loses that cycle to the reactive path. It never comes back, and for the rest of the run the room is above its band more than half the time.

**MPC no longer ends a cycle it is still planning on a borrowed slope.** That holds against an idle that scores better and against MPC's preference for idle. The borrowing lasts until the cycle's own run can stand (see below): about fifteen minutes at a battery sensor's five-minute cadence, ten at a sample a minute. The cycle then leaves a slope for the next one. Three things still end a held cycle:
- The room reaching the band edge the cycle is driving it towards: the bottom for cooling, the top for heating. The cycle is released at the first refresh that sees the room at or past that edge, so it is released within one refresh of reaching it (the unit's own lag then carries the room on a little, as after any release).
- MPC not being ready, or deferring, which hands the decision to the reactive path. The reactive path is never held.
- The cycle's run going more than 17 minutes without a sample. Nothing is recorded for a mode command that raises, and a cloud unit can take the command all the same, so a held cycle re-commanded through such a fault would otherwise stop growing and stay held, cooling the room to the bottom of its band and then flipping between cool and idle there. After that long a run borrows nothing, just as a run resumed after a gap of that length borrows nothing, and the cycle ends: by the reactive path, or by MPC itself when the other recovery slope keeps it ready.

The hold only stops MPC ending a cycle. A switch to the other action, say ahead of a band about to rise past a cooling room, is not held. As with any reversal, though, the coordinator's cross-mode gate keeps the unit on the action it was running for up to `cross_mode_min_minutes` before it commands the new one, and a held cycle makes that more likely to be a heat or cool action than idle. The hold keys on the slope rather than on a clock, so nothing new is remembered and it holds equally at any cadence.

**A young cycle's own fit now also waits ten minutes.** At about a sample a minute, a new run had the four samples a fit needs three minutes in, before many units have moved the room at all. Its fit came out the wrong way round, the sign guard discarded it, and MPC lost the cycle at the fourth minute: a known limit of v0.21.0, pinned by a test. A run that is still running now also has to have been watched for 10 minutes (`CARRY_OVER_MIN_SPAN_MINUTES`) before its own fit replaces the borrowed slope. Until then that fit is set aside, whichever way round it came out. Once the run has ended, its own fit stands, as before: it is all there is to say about that cycle, and the next cycle borrows it anyway. The sign guard still judges every fit MPC uses: the borrowed one passed it when the previous cycle was fitted, and the run's own is judged once it takes over. At a five-minute cadence four samples normally span about fifteen minutes, so this rarely matters there: in the ten days of history, extra samples gave a running cycle four inside ten minutes four times, and two of those changed a decision. A unit still not moving the room after ten minutes loses the cycle then.

**The walk back, revisited.** v0.21.0 lent only the previous cycle's slope, not the last one long enough to fit, because walking further back let MPC chatter. With the hold, MPC's own cycles run long enough to lend unless something else ends them first, so a short run now comes from the band edge, a reversal to the other action (not held), MPC deferring to the reactive path, the reactive path's own cycles, or a hand edit. Measured again with the hold in place, the walk back gained at most half a point of time in band (with no lag, at a sample a minute) and 0.1–0.2 points on the rebound plant at five minutes, and changed nothing elsewhere, so the previous cycle alone still lends.

**What you'll notice.** On a zone on MPC, a cycle MPC starts runs until it has a slope of its own, unless the room reaches the band's far edge first or one of the other exits above ends it. That is about 15 to 20 minutes at a five-minute cadence, and `mpc_ready` stays on through it. Expect more time in band and the unit running somewhat more; at a five-minute cadence, fewer and longer cycles too. At a sample a minute, MPC is in control more of the time, and starts can rise a little where the unit is slow to come up (figures below). At about a sample a minute, `sensor.{zone}_thermal_slope` shows `method_recovery_*: previous` for a cycle's first ten minutes, even once its run has four samples. A zone not on MPC controls exactly as before, though its `mpc_action` shadow sensor can show the hold. What remains is the switching itself, after cycles that have run to a fit. In simulation MPC still restarted the action within 15 minutes after 15–21 % of the cycles it ended, at a five-minute cadence (17–49 % before). The recorded restarts above were all of that kind, after cycles running on slopes of their own, which the hold leaves alone.

**Measured.** The five zones' recorded history (49 zone-days, 13,275 refreshes) was replayed through hysteresis, the predictor and MPC on v0.21.0 and on this release.
- All 307 refreshes with MPC off decided exactly as before, and the reactive predictor's decision was unchanged at every refresh.
- 30 MPC decisions changed. 28 were holds of a cycle v0.21.0 would have ended on a borrowed slope.
- The other 2 were the span: cool cycles that extra samples on a five-minute zone had given four samples inside ten minutes. v0.21.0 ended one on its own young fit, and lost the other when that fit came out the wrong way round.

A replay can't say what the room would have done once a decision changed, so each of the 97 recorded MPC starts was also run forward three hours in the v0.21.0 simulation, with the real decision code and min-cycle gates in charge, against five ways a room might answer its unit:
- the first-order lag of about 3.5 minutes fitted for v0.21.0;
- no lag;
- a dead time of 1.5 minutes then a 2-minute lag;
- a dead time of 3 minutes then a 1-minute lag;
- a room that rebounds after a release, as the recorded rooms do.

The rebound model was fitted per zone from 290 recorded releases, against the idle slope MPC was planning with. After a cool cycle of ten minutes or less, the room rose a median 0.2 °C more over the next ten minutes than that slope said it would. The 3.5-minute lag plant reproduces v0.21.0's published figures, but on the lag-only plants MPC restarted within 15 minutes after only 19–41 % of the cycles it ended (at 293 s), against 78 % recorded. The rebound plant brings that to 49 %.

Starts went from 0.71 to 0.59 an hour, the median cycle from 10 to 20 minutes, and MPC was ready two thirds of the time instead of 43 %. The price is runtime: a cycle held to a fit cools or heats further past the switching point, and the unit ran 24.6 % of the time instead of 19.6 %. For scale, the same starts with MPC off spent 40 % of the time in band with the unit running 12.8 % of the time, and v0.20.0 spent 69 % at 18.5 %.

Four alternatives were measured and not taken. Ranges are time in band across the five plants at a five-minute cadence, against 73–81 % for v0.21.0 and 88–90 % for the hold.

**v0.21.0** lets MPC run the cycles it starts. Until now it could not: on the very next refresh, a heat or cool cycle MPC had started was handed to the reactive predictor, which released it at once whenever the room was inside its band. In production on 24 Sep a zone on MPC, cooling only at the time, started a cool cycle at 09:26:37 and released it at 09:27:35, 58 seconds later. The same happened at 12:33 and 13:36 that day, and `binary_sensor.{zone}_mpc_ready` went off with each one.

**Why it happened.** Every cycle starts as a run of one sample, and each recovery slope comes from the latest run of its action alone. So the refresh after MPC started a cycle found no recovery slope for it. The earlier cycle MPC had planned the start with was still in the buffer; it just wasn't the latest run any more. With no other recovery slope, MPC was no longer ready and the reactive predictor took over. With one, the action just started dropped out of MPC's candidates. Either way a room inside its band was released at once: by hysteresis, which releases cooling at the band's upper edge and heating at its lower one, or by MPC itself, left with nothing to plan the cycle with. The min-cycle gate never comes into it, because idle releases deliberately pass through so a cycle can always stop. The effect outlasted the cycle, too: the one-sample run went on hiding the earlier one, so MPC stayed not ready until a reactive cycle long enough to fit replaced it. v0.19.0 removed what prompted the starts that day, the phantom idle slope, but not the mechanism. In ten days of five zones' recorded history MPC started 97 cycles and lost the recovery slope on the refresh after every one of them, 63 of them after v0.19.0 was installed. Hysteresis released 59 of those cycles on that refresh. The other 38 carried on only because the room was still outside its band. After each start MPC then sat out a median of 68 minutes.

**A cycle too young for a recovery slope of its own now plans with the previous cycle's.** That is the slope MPC started the cycle with, and it is used until the new cycle has the four samples it needs for its own. It is borrowed only from the run of that action just before the new one, and only if that run is long enough to fit. That fit goes through the v0.15.0 sign guard like any other, so a previous cycle whose fit was discarded lends nothing, and a new run long enough to fit always keeps its own slope, or the guard's verdict on it. Only a new cycle borrows. A young run whose sample before it was of its own action, split from it only by a gap in sampling, is a cycle resumed after an outage. v0.20.0 decided that such a cycle waits for a slope of its own, and that decision stands. A gap between the two cycles doesn't stop the previous cycle lending its slope, whether it falls in the idle between them or straight before the new cycle's first sample, as after a restart while the zone idled. That matches v0.20.0, which left standing the slope of a cycle that ended before an outage. A gap inside the previous cycle splits it, as v0.20.0 does, and only the part after the gap can lend. Nothing is persisted: the previous cycle has to be one the sample buffer still holds, and the buffer is pruned to 90 minutes as samples are appended. This is for MPC only, like the cached idle slope: the reactive predictor and hysteresis use the live slopes, so a zone not on MPC controls exactly as before (its MPC shadow, `mpc_action` and `mpc_ready`, and `sensor.{zone}_thermal_slope` do show the carried value). On `sensor.{zone}_thermal_slope` a carried value shows with `method_recovery_heat` / `method_recovery_cool` reading `previous`. `sample_count_recovery_*` and `std_dev_recovery_*` still describe the young run, as the idle counts do beside a cached idle slope.

**Only the previous cycle, deliberately.** MPC has no hysteresis of its own around the temperature at which it switches between running and idle. Near that point it can end a cycle after a refresh or two and start the next a few minutes later: in the history it restarted within 15 minutes after 81 of the 104 cycles it ended itself. A cycle that short has no slope to lend. Through the idle after it the cycle before it still lends, so MPC can start the next cycle on that. But the next cycle finds only the short one before it, and the reactive path takes it, as before, which keeps MPC's chatter short. Walking back past such cycles to the last one long enough to fit was measured too, and it lets the chatter run for as long as that cycle stays in the buffer. At these zones' five-minute cadence it did best of the carry-over variants, but at a one-minute cadence it nearly doubled the very thing this release is about: cycles of two and a half minutes or less went from 98 to 182 (figures below). The proper fix for that is switching hysteresis in MPC, which is left for a release of its own.

**What you'll notice.** On a zone on MPC, a cycle MPC starts after one long enough to fit now runs until MPC ends it, instead of for one refresh, and `mpc_ready` goes off far less often. `sensor.{zone}_thermal_slope` shows a recovery slope for the first few samples of such a cycle, where it used to read unknown. MPC is in control more of the time, so expect somewhat more cycle starts and more time in band (figures below). `mpc_ready` can still go off in four cases, the first three at the start of a cycle. First, the cycle before it may have no slope to lend, either because it was too short to fit (usually a cycle MPC ended itself) or because its fit was discarded. Second, the previous cycle may no longer be in the buffer. That happened once in the 97 starts, when the run MPC had started with lost its oldest sample to the window between one refresh and the next. Third, the cycle may have resumed after an outage, as in v0.20.0. Fourth, the carry-over ends when the young cycle can be fitted, and at about a sample a minute that is three minutes in. A unit that hasn't moved the room by then fits flat or the wrong way round. The sign guard discards that fit, nothing is carried over a rejected one, and the reactive path takes the cycle at its fourth minute rather than its first. This doesn't happen at these zones' five-minute cadence, where the fourth sample comes a quarter of an hour in.

**Measured.** The five zones' recorded history (49 zone-days, 13,275 refreshes) was replayed through hysteresis, the predictor and MPC. The replay reproduces 99.94 % of the decisions those zones recorded and 99.98 % of their `mpc_ready` states. At all 59 releases the fix kept the recovery slope, and MPC then went on with the cycle 47 times and ended it itself 12 times. MPC was sidelined after one start in 97 instead of after every start. At the refresh after a start, the run carried over was a median of 15 minutes old (68 at most). Each carried slope was compared with what the new cycle went on to fit for itself. For cooling it was within a median 0.3–0.4 °C/h. For heating it was within a median 0.7 °C/h of the cycle's final slope, and further (1.9 °C/h) from its first fit, which covers the minutes in which the unit is still coming up to output. A replay can't say what the room would have done once a decision changed, so each of the 97 starts was also run forward three hours in a simulation. In it the room moves at the zone's own learned slopes, the unit's output lags its command by about 3.5 minutes (fitted from 80 recorded cycles), and the real decision code and min-cycle gates are in charge. At these zones' five-minute cadence the share of time in band rose from 69 % to 80 %, and the degree-minutes outside the band fell by 27 %. Cycle starts went from 0.61 to 0.71 an hour, and the unit ran 19.6 % of the time instead of 18.5 %. Cycles of six minutes or less fell from 50 % of all cycles to 41 %, and `mpc_ready` changed state 0.40 times an hour instead of 0.62. At a one-minute cadence, cycles of two and a half minutes or less fell from 98 to 65, starts went from 0.69 to 0.87 an hour, and time in band rose from 68 % to 79 %. How the unit comes up matters, so the lag was also tried as a dead time before a quicker rise. At the five-minute cadence nothing changed: time in band rose by 11 to 12 points for every response shape tried, with no cycle lost at its fourth sample. At a one-minute cadence the gain was 11 to 12 points for a unit that responds gradually, but 7 points for one that does nothing for three minutes. For that unit 83 held cycles were lost at their fourth sample, as described above, though the cycles of two and a half minutes or less still fell from 98 to 12. With no lag at all and a one-minute cadence, those shortest cycles rose from 114 to 191, because lag is what damps MPC's own switching. Three alternatives were measured and not taken. Walking back past short cycles to the last one long enough to fit did best of the carry-over variants at the five-minute cadence, with 82 % in band, 34 % short cycles and 0.77 starts an hour. At a one-minute cadence, though, it took the cycles of two and a half minutes or less from 98 to 182 and starts from 0.69 to 1.35 an hour, and with no lag those cycles reached 970. Holding a cycle MPC started for the min-cycle dwell does nothing for readiness at a five-minute cadence: after 96 of the 97 starts the cycle still had no slope of its own when the eight minutes ran out. Persisting recovery slopes, as the idle slope is persisted, kept MPC ready throughout the simulation and gave the best time in band, 92 %, but at 1.13 starts an hour, with half of all cycles six minutes or shorter.

**Upgrading.** Nothing to do. The buffer restored after the restart that installs this is used as it stands.

**v0.20.0** stops a slope being fitted across an outage. The sample buffer only grows while the zone can see what it is learning — the room sensor reporting, the climate entity reachable (a dropped command appends nothing, since v0.17.0; only shadow mode and the min-cycle gates sample on regardless), Home Assistant running — and when any of those stops, the buffer simply has a hole in it. The predictor joined the runs on either side of the hole by action label alone, so a slope could be fitted across an hour in which nothing was seen. In production on 24 Sep a cool run from 10:45 to 11:10 was joined to a sample at 12:13, across the hour in which the climate entity was unreachable; the unit had kept cooling, so the recovery slope came out right by luck.

**Why it matters.** A fit across a gap is set by whatever happened in the gap. Its two clusters sit far apart on the time axis, which gives the older one the leverage to tilt the line however little weight its age leaves it. A room that stepped down 0.6 °C during a 25-minute sensor outage, and held level on both sides of it, read as cooling at up to 0.9 °C/h, fading over the next forty minutes — live, and written to the cache stamped as measured after the outage, so a zone that left idle in that time could have planned with it until the next settled idle slope replaced it, for up to a day. A cool run whose unit lost power along with its network connection, and came back on its own auto-restart a degree warmer, fitted as cooling that barely moved the room: its recovery slope was rejected as sign-wrong and then came out too shallow, until the samples from before the outage had aged out of the window — half an hour in which MPC either sat out or planned with the wrong rate.

**A run now stops at any gap of more than 17 minutes between consecutive samples.** A zone samples whenever its room reading changes, at most once a minute — every 293 seconds for the battery sensors this was measured on. In ten days of five zones' recorded history, every gap inside a run with nothing dark was 14.7 minutes or less (two lost reports), and every longer one coincided with the room sensor or the climate entity being dark. Seventeen sits between two and three lost reports from a sensor reporting every five minutes, so neither lands on the edge — fifteen, the first guess, sits exactly on two of them. A gap of exactly 17 minutes is still spanned. Nothing is specific to the cause: Home Assistant being down leaves a gap like any other outage. The v0.18.0 flush when a stand-in changes the action stays, and still matters after a long outage: a stand-in's cycle leaves no samples, so for as long as the idle samples from before the outage are still in the 90-minute window, the idle on either side of the cycle would otherwise be one idle stretch — joined into one run, or split but settled from before the cycle (see below) — and the cycle's aftermath would be fitted as passive drift.

**What you'll notice.** After an outage, a heat or cool run that resumes starts again, as at the start of any cycle: it needs four samples for a recovery slope — twenty minutes with a sensor reporting every five — and if that was the only recovery slope in the window, MPC runs the reactive predictor until then. An idle stretch that resumes is fitted again from what is sampled after the outage, once there are four samples. Its settle window is still measured from where the stretch began, so an outage straight after a release still waits out the aftermath, while one in the middle of a long idle stretch costs twenty minutes at a five-minute cadence. That assumes the unit idled through the outage. A stand-in's cycle cannot break the assumption, because it flushes the buffer, but a cycle hidden by a mode call that fails yet still reaches the unit (a cloud timeout, see v0.17.1) can: its aftermath is then fitted as idle drift, as it was before this release. After an outage of about 50 minutes or more the slope drops out once more: when the last samples from before the outage leave the 90-minute window, the stretch starts after the outage, and so does its settle window, until four samples are past it — straight away after an outage longer than the window. Meanwhile the cached idle slope stands in for MPC; the reactive predictor, which only ever uses the live slope, does no idle-based anticipation until there is one again. All of this happens even when the unit did keep doing what it was doing, as on 24 Sep: nothing in the buffer tells the two apart. A run that ended before the outage is untouched — if the zone comes back doing something else, the cycle before the outage is still a whole, watched cycle and its slope stands. Zones that never lose sight of their room see no change at all: wherever no two consecutive samples are more than 17 minutes apart, every slope is computed exactly as before. The exception is a room sensor whose reading changes less often than that with nothing wrong: Home Assistant passes on only a change, so a coarse sensor reporting in 0.5 °C steps, with no humidity sensor to prompt refreshes, can go quiet for that long. Its runs are split at every such stretch. A heat or cool cycle that moves the room by less than one step every 17 minutes — for 0.5 °C steps, slower than about 1.8 °C/h — now never gets a recovery slope (before, it got one only an hour or more into the cycle), so neither anticipatory shutoff nor MPC has one to work with during it; and an idle stretch that quiet gets no live idle slope at all. None of the five zones replayed below has such a sensor. On `sensor.{zone}_thermal_slope`, the counts for a run that carried on across a gap describe only what was sampled since it.

**Measured.** Ten days of five zones' recorded history — 47 zone-days, 10,700 gaps between samples — replayed through hysteresis, the predictor and MPC with and without the split; the replay reproduces 1,228 of the 1,242 decisions those zones recorded after v0.19.0 was installed. Fifteen outages fell inside a run and inside the window: the room sensor dark for 18 to 57 minutes, or the climate entity unreachable across gaps of 54 and 64 minutes. In most of them the room drifted at much the same rate on both sides, and the joined fit did little harm. One was not like that: a room that rose 0.6 °C during a 20-minute sensor outage read as warming at up to 1.5 °C/h for most of the next hour, against the 0.4 °C/h seen after it. Over the fourteen idle outages — thirteen with enough samples after them to tell what the room did next — the idle slope MPC planned with in the hour after each (the live one, or the cache while there was none) came out a little closer to what the room then did — a mean error of 0.11 °C/h split, against 0.12 joined — and much closer for that 20-minute outage: 0.18 against 0.41. Three decisions differed in the 47 zone-days, all on the 24 Sep cool run: with no recovery slope for the resumed cycle, MPC was not ready for 19 minutes — the only time in the 47 zone-days that the split cost a zone its MPC readiness — and the reactive predictor released a room that MPC, on the joined fit, would have gone on cooling though it was already inside its band. The live idle slope was withheld for 170 minutes in all while samples accumulated after outages. Starting the settle window again after every gap was measured too, and was worse on both counts: 521 minutes withheld, and a mean error of 0.18 °C/h, more than the join's.

**Upgrading.** Nothing to do. The split applies to the buffer as it stands, including the one restored after the restart that installs it. A restart leaves a gap of its own — the downtime, plus up to five minutes of samples taken before it but not yet written to disk, plus the wait for the first reading after it — so if it takes more than about ten minutes, a heat or cool run in progress starts again after it.

**v0.19.0** stops the idle slope learning the aftermath of the HVAC's own cycle as the room's passive drift. The incident: a zone on apparent temperature spent an evening flip-flopping between `cool` and `fan_only`, and late on a 5 °C night its MPC was cooling a room that sat inside its band, convinced the room warmed on its own at 1.5 °C/h. It did not: across the samples that value was fitted from, the raw temperature moved 0.04 °C/h.

**Where the phantom drift came from.** An idle run nearly always begins at the release of a heat or cool cycle, and for a while after that the room is still answering the cycle rather than drifting. The air relaxes back toward walls and furniture the cycle never reached; and after cooling, `fan_only` blows across a coil still wet with condensate and puts the water back into the room. With `use_apparent_temperature` on, humidity reads as warmth — about 0.08 °C of apparent temperature per point of RH at 20 °C — so a four-point rebound in fifteen minutes fitted as +1.5 °C/h. A zone that cycles every twenty minutes only ever sees that aftermath, and the loop feeds itself: the predictor starts the next cool cycle early, MPC holds cooling inside the band, and each cycle dries the air for the next idle stretch to rebound from. It is not peculiar to cooling or to apparent temperature. Across ten days of two zones' recorded history, the first idle slope after a cool release had a median of +1.4 °C/h, and after a heat release −0.9 °C/h (the air falling back toward cooler walls) — against about ±0.25 °C/h half an hour later.

**The idle slope now leaves out the first 30 minutes of every idle run.** Measured from the run's first sample in the buffer, whatever came before it: once the cycle in front of a run has aged out of the 90-minute window nothing says the run began at a release, and trimming only runs with a visible release was measured letting exactly those back in, transient intact. The price is paid by a run that has idled for longer than the window: it loses its oldest half hour — under 4 % of the fit's weight but about a third of its leverage — so its slope is somewhat noisier (in the replay below such slopes moved by a median 0.03 °C/h). Recovery slopes are fitted whole, as before — the cycle is what they measure. On `sensor.{zone}_thermal_slope`, `sample_count_idle` and `std_dev_idle` now describe the samples actually behind the idle estimate, so they read 0 for the first half hour of each idle stretch. No plausibility cap was added: once settled, genuine idle slopes still reach 1.2–1.75 °C/h (morning sun), so a cap tight enough to have caught the incident would clip real ones.

**The persisted idle slope is stamped with when it was measured.** v0.12.0 stamped it with the time of the refresh that last recomputed it, and a slope stays live for as long as its run is in the window — so an idle run from before a cool cycle went on being written back as new. It got worse when the climate entity became unreachable: every command is dropped and nothing is sampled, so the buffer froze, nothing aged out of it, and the same value was re-stamped for the whole outage. In the incident an idle slope last measured at 20:40 came back as the cache at 22:18 reading "9.7 min old", good until the following evening. The stamp is now the newest sample behind the value, and nothing is rewritten until something new has been measured; `idle_slope_cached_age_min` counts from that sample.

**What you'll notice.** Every idle stretch now has to last the 30-minute settle window plus four samples before it yields a live idle slope — about 35 minutes with a sensor that reports every minute, 50 with one that reports every five (it used to be four samples: 4 and 20 minutes). A sensor that reports less often than about every 20 minutes — one that reports only on a change, in a room drifting slowly — never leaves four samples in the hour of window that remains after the settle, so it gets no live idle slope at all. Until then MPC runs on the cached value if there is one; the reactive predictor, which only ever uses the live value, simply does no idle-based anticipation during that time. A zone whose idle stretches are *all* shorter than that measures no new idle slope: it keeps its cached one while that lasts (24 hours), and with none — after install, a manual-edit flush or a sensor swap (both clear it), or a day with no long idle — it runs the reactive predictor rather than MPC. That is deliberate (a phantom idle slope is what the incident was), and in the history replayed below it never came close: both zones had an idle stretch of 50 minutes or more every day, the longest each day between three and twenty-one hours, though two in three of their idle stretches were shorter. Replaying ten days of those two zones' recorded history through the old and new estimators, open loop, decisions differed at 4.8 % and 2.6 % of refreshes, almost all from cool or heat to idle; `mpc_ready` was unchanged; and the share of refreshes steering by an idle slope steeper than 1 °C/h fell from 13 % to 1 % and from 21 % to 2 %.

**Upgrading.** An idle slope persisted by an earlier version is kept, and if it was one of these phantom values, MPC goes on planning with it until the zone next idles long enough to measure a settled one — which, on a zone MPC keeps cycling, can be most of its remaining 24 hours. Upgrading while the zone is in a long idle stretch replaces it on the first refresh; otherwise, if a zone was misbehaving this way, keep `switch.{zone}_mpc_enabled` off until `sensor.{zone}_thermal_slope` has shown `idle_slope_source: live` with the zone's own room sensor reporting and `learning_enabled` on — a zone on a stand-in reading, or not learning, persists nothing.

**v0.18.0** lets a zone keep controlling while its room sensor is dark. v0.16.0 made that outage *visible*; nothing yet did anything about it, and the incident behind this release is the one its docs predicted: two battery-powered Thread sensors dropped off the mesh (the mains-powered Thread routers they hung off had been switched off), both zones had just been parked in `fan_only` by their own readings, and with no reading to control from they commanded nothing for the rest of the night. Rooms at 15 °C in the morning against a 20 °C band.

**A stand-in reading now takes over after a grace period.** Once the configured sensor has been unavailable for a continuous five minutes, the zone reads from — in order — an optional **fallback temperature sensor** (new in the zone's *Configure* dialog), then the climate entity's own `current_temperature`, which almost every climate entity exposes and which is allowed unless you switch it off in the same dialog. Control resumes from that reading and stops the moment the configured sensor reports again. Inside the grace window, and when no stand-in has a reading either — a unit that publishes no `current_temperature` included — control is unchanged from before: no reading, no command, and the `source` attribute reads `none`; in the second case the log now says that the stand-in never engaged (see below). The window is deliberate — a sensor on a weak mesh drops for seconds at a time, and swapping sources at each blip would feed hysteresis a step between two readings with different offsets. A zone with no schedule has nothing else to wake it, so the grace period runs on its own timer, and while the climate's reading is in use a change in *that* is what triggers a decision.

**What a stand-in does not do.** It runs plain hysteresis, with both deadbands widened by 0.5 °C: the unit's own sensor sits at the indoor head and usually reports whole degrees, so deadbands tuned for a room sensor would short-cycle on it, and the learned thermal model describes the *configured* sensor's placement, so the predictor's anticipation and MPC's plan — both of which extrapolate from the current reading — are bypassed (the shadow `predicted_action` / `mpc_action` sensors still show what they would do). Nothing is sampled from a stand-in, for the same reason the *Configure* dialog flushes the buffer on a sensor swap: sampling resumes with the configured sensor. The buffer is flushed if the stand-in changes the HVAC action, too — the predictor joins runs by action alone, so the samples on either side of the outage would otherwise be read as one run spanning a real heat or cool cycle, and the idle slope would come out as a strong drift that never happened. Only then, though: a stand-in that merely holds the last action leaves the buffer alone, so a restart on which the sensor is slow to publish — Thread or Matter after a power cut, a deep-sleep ESPHome node — keeps the model restored from disk. The persisted idle slope is kept: it was learned from the configured sensor and is still good.

**What you'll see.** `sensor.{zone}_room_temperature` mirrors whichever reading is in use and gains a `source` attribute (`primary` / `fallback_sensor` / `climate` / `none`). `binary_sensor.{zone}_room_sensor_unavailable` is unchanged and stays `on` for the whole outage — it is about the device that needs attention, and a stand-in is a workaround, not a fix — so an alert on it is still worth having. The log gains a `WARNING` when a stand-in takes over (naming the sensor, how long it has been dark, and what is standing in), another if the stand-in itself goes dark, and an `INFO` when the configured sensor takes back over; a stand-in that flaps is throttled the same way as the sensor edge, to one line per direction per five minutes. The existing outage warning now says a stand-in is coming, when one is allowed — and if the grace period then ends with nothing to read (a unit that publishes no `current_temperature`, or the fallback sensor dark too with the climate reading switched off), a further `WARNING` says so and names what was tried, once per outage, so the record does not end on a hand-over that never happened. Zones that never lose their sensor are untouched: no new refreshes, no new samples, same decisions.

**v0.17.1** finishes v0.17.0 and fixes a flush it left in place.

**`climate.set_hvac_mode` is guarded at last.** v0.17.0 named this as its known limitation: a cloud unit that timed out on the mode call raised into Home Assistant's background task, so the only trace was an anonymous `Task exception was never retrieved` with no zone, entity or mode in it — once per refresh, for the length of the fault. It is now caught and reported like the other two calls, and names one thing they do not: a throttled `WARNING` giving the zone, the entity, the mode it was trying to set, the traceback, and the class of the exception — which is what separates a cloud timeout from a unit that will never accept the mode. Nothing is recorded, so the zone retries on the next refresh. Unlike the unreachable case below, those retries do reach the climate integration — nothing filters them out — so a unit whose cloud API is rate limited will see one call per refresh for as long as the fault lasts. The throttle is one line per five minutes for a fault that simply persists; a unit that alternates between failing and succeeding gets one per episode, which is louder — that is the same trade the other command warnings make, so a return is announced promptly.

What it deliberately does **not** do is guess whether the command arrived. A raise says the call did not complete; it does not say the unit never got it, and on a cloud round-trip that timed out on its confirming poll it usually did. Six ways of acting on that guess were tried and each was measured either flushing the learned model on a unit that had taken the mode, or swallowing a thermostat edit made during the fault. So the guard changes nothing else, which means the pre-existing behaviour is unchanged too: **a manual edit made at the wall while the mode call is failing can still be absorbed rather than detected.** It happens whenever the zone retries at or faster than the 30-second echo window, which covers most installations: a room sensor reporting every 30 seconds re-arms the window exactly as it expires, so edits are absorbed at essentially every offset, and one reporting faster is worse. Above 30 seconds the exposure falls off gradually rather than stopping — roughly the fraction `(period − 30) / period` of edits are caught, so a 60-second sensor catches about half. It needs a change to the manual-edit detector rather than to the command path.

**The fan and setpoint warnings now carry their traceback too.** Both previously logged a single line naming the call that failed; the exception under it is what makes a misbehaving climate platform identifiable rather than merely named. If you have a unit with a permanent fault of that kind — a thermostat advertising only a temperature range makes the setpoint call raise on every refresh, which is the case v0.17.0 called out — the five-minute warning you were already getting now has a traceback under it. Same rate, more text.

One more thing worth knowing if your unit does not advertise `fan_only`: Home Assistant 2025.4 turns an unadvertised mode from a warning into an error, and Comfort Band commands `fan_only` on every idle release. On that combination every release fails permanently. Heat and cool commands still land, so the zone goes on heating and goes on learning heat samples. What fails is the release itself: the unit is never taken out of heat when the room reaches band. And because a release that raises records no sample, the zone never gets an *idle* one either, so the idle slope never arrives and `mpc_ready` stays off — the predictive path never starts on such a unit. The warning is there to make that visible; the fix is not in this release.

**A setpoint that disappears is no longer read as somebody editing the thermostat.** `state_attributes` publishes `temperature` only while the entity advertises `TARGET_TEMPERATURE`, and several integrations vary that by mode — so a unit reaching `fan_only`, which Comfort Band commands on **every idle release**, simply stops reporting a target. v0.17.0 read that as a hand edit and flushed the sample buffer and the persisted idle slope on the release it had just asked for — whenever the unit reported the change late enough to miss the 30-second echo window, which a polling or cloud-backed one does routinely: measured at 10–14 flushes an hour on such a unit, with the learned model never accumulating. An absent target is now treated as an absence of information rather than a value, both when comparing and when recording the baseline, so those zones keep their model. Nobody can clear a dial, so this costs almost nothing. What it does accept is any target that disappears while the mode stays where Comfort Band expects it — moving a dual-mode unit from a single target to a temperature *range* is one route there, a preset that switches it to a range is another.

Also: an apply whose climate call outlives the next refresh no longer hands back an echo-window timestamp that a later, successful command has since replaced.

**v0.17.0** fixes three bugs on the path that actually commands the HVAC and records what it was told — the bookkeeping every dwell gate and every later decision then trusts.

**(1) The zone recorded its action only after the setpoint call.** `last_action` was written after `climate.set_temperature`, so anything that raised in between left the unit conditioning while the store still said otherwise. That is not a hypothetical: an entity advertising only `TARGET_TEMPERATURE_RANGE` — Ecobee, Nest, many `heat_cool` mini-splits — makes Home Assistant itself raise for a plain `temperature`, so on that whole class of hardware every heat/cool entry raised and `last_action` never became correct at all. The commitment now rests on `set_hvac_mode` alone, which is the call that makes the unit start conditioning; the fan and setpoint calls that follow are best-effort, each warning (throttled) instead of escaping into the background task.

**(2) A clean return from a service call was treated as delivery.** Home Assistant answers a call it cannot deliver by skipping the entity and returning normally, so a climate that was `unavailable` at dispatch silently swallowed the command — and the zone recorded it anyway. That armed the min-cycle gate, so the command was **never retried**, even once the unit came back. The zone now records nothing when the unit was unreachable on both sides of the call, says so once per 5 minutes, and retries on the next refresh.

**(3) A slow unit's own echo was read as somebody editing the thermostat.** The manual-edit detector compared the climate against Comfort Band's *intent*, so a cloud-backed or otherwise lagging unit — whose state still shows the previous mode when the call returns — mismatched as soon as its real echo arrived outside the 30 s window. That flushed the sample buffer and cleared the persisted idle slope on **every single command** to such a unit, taking `mpc_ready` with it. The detector now tracks what the entity is reporting and what was asked for as two separate things, accepting either while the unit catches up. Same release stops an entity going `unavailable` or `unknown` from counting as an edit — a bridge blip is not somebody at the wall, and it was wiping the model on every dropout.

Measured over a simulated hour against v0.16.0, with a unit whose echo lands a minute late: buffer flushes 9 → 0 and samples retained 1 → 57. A healthy, promptly-echoing zone is unchanged on every count — same refreshes, store writes, service calls and samples.

**What you'll notice:** new `WARNING` lines (throttled to one per fault per 5 minutes) when a command can't be delivered, or when a unit refuses a setpoint or fan mode; no samples recorded while the climate is unreachable, since we don't know what an unreachable unit is doing; and a zone whose climate is offline re-issuing its command on each refresh until it lands — those calls are filtered out inside Home Assistant and never reach the device. **Known limitation at the time (fixed in v0.17.1):** `climate.set_hvac_mode` itself was still unguarded, so a cloud unit that timed out on that call raised into the background task as it did before.

**v0.16.0** makes losing a room sensor **visible**. A zone whose temperature sensor goes unavailable can't control at all — the decider has no reading, so nothing decides — and until now that was completely silent: no log line, no entity, nothing in the UI. Diagnosed from a master bedroom that drifted several degrees below its band for hours after its Matter/Thread sensor dropped off the mesh, with nothing anywhere to say the zone had stopped controlling.

A new **`binary_sensor.{zone}_room_sensor_unavailable`** (device class `problem`, first-class rather than diagnostic) turns that into something you can notify on, and the sensor's availability edge is now logged, naming the entity to check. Pair the sensor with a `for:` of a few minutes to ride out routine blips:

```yaml
- trigger: state
  entity_id: binary_sensor.mbr_room_sensor_unavailable
  to: "on"
  for: "00:05:00"
```

Three details worth knowing. The log is rate-limited — one line per direction per five minutes, so at most two — because a dying battery or a weak mesh link crosses that boundary hundreds of times an hour. It compares against what was last *logged* rather than against reality, so a throttled edge is re-offered on the next refresh instead of being dropped. That is a narrowing, not a guarantee: a link that blips and then dies inside one window can leave the log's last word reading "reporting again", because a dead sensor produces no further refresh to re-offer on. The binary sensor is `on` throughout in every one of these cases, which is why it, and not the log, is the thing to automate against. The outage line drops to info for a zone still in shadow mode, since a zone that commands nothing hasn't stopped controlling, and nothing is logged at all while Home Assistant is still starting, when a sensor's own integration may simply not have loaded yet. A non-finite reading — `nan` or `±inf` — now counts as no reading. `nan` parses as a float but compares false against everything, so hysteresis simply latches whatever it was already doing: a heating zone heats indefinitely, an idle one idles while the room drifts, and `−inf` commands heat outright. In every case nothing alerted, because the sensor looked like it was reporting. And the entity deliberately stays available on stale data when a refresh fails, but only in the alarming direction — a stale `on` is worth keeping, while a stale `off` would assert "no problem" about a room that has since gone dark.

**Scope note.** This makes the failure *visible*; it does not change what the zone does about it. A zone that goes blind mid-cycle keeps running whatever was last commanded, with the head unit's own thermostat still regulating to that setpoint — the behaviour before this release. Releasing the cycle automatically turned out to need considerably more care than it appears to (seventeen review rounds' worth), so it is deferred rather than rushed.

**v0.15.0** fixes an MPC failure mode where a zone could **idle while sitting several degrees below its band**. The learned recovery slopes (how fast the room warms while heating / cools while cooling) are WLS-fit per action segment. A zone that only ever gets brief, sub-minute heat blips (e.g. a slow-to-heat gym chasing a steep morning band) accumulates just a handful of noisy heat samples whose fit comes out **negative** — i.e. the model believes *heating cools the room*. MPC's forward simulation then projects the `heat` candidate downward and `idle` upward and picks **idle**, stranding the room below band (and starving the estimator of the clean heat samples that would correct the slope — a self-reinforcing loop). v0.15.0 adds a **sign guard**: a heating segment can't have a non-positive slope and a cooling segment can't have a non-negative one, so such an estimate is discarded (`recovery_slope_heat`/`_cool` → `None`, `method_* = "rejected"` on `sensor.{zone}_thermal_slope`). With the slope gone, MPC falls back to the reactive predictor, which heats when below band — so the room starts closing the gap immediately instead of idling. Idle (passive-drift) slopes keep both signs. **Behaviour change for MPC zones:** where a wrong-sign recovery slope was previously trusted, the zone now heats/cools toward band instead of idling; zones with clean slope data are unaffected. (Diagnosed live from a gym that sat ~5 °C under its morning band for an hour while `recovery_slope_heat` read −0.1 to −0.9 °C/hr.)

**v0.14.1** is a fast-follow hardening of v0.14.0, two fixes. (1) **Profile rename/delete/clone now track shared schedules.** Renaming a profile (e.g. `home`→`casa`) already re-keyed each zone's own schedules; it now does the same for the per-profile slots *inside* shared schedules — so a zone assigned to a shared schedule keeps resolving the shared band instead of silently dropping to its manual band. Deleting a (non-default) profile likewise clears its slot from shared schedules, and cloning a profile seeds the cloned slot into them (an independent copy), exactly as it already did for each zone's own schedules. (2) **Transition-time validation tightened.** The `at`-field regex previously admitted impossible hours `24:00`–`29:59`, which passed the schema and then raised a raw `ValueError` deep in parsing; it now rejects them cleanly at the schema layer, with a defence-in-depth parser that surfaces a `ServiceValidationError` for any value that bypasses the schema (pre-existing on `main`, unrelated to shared schedules). **v0.14.0** adds **named shared schedules** so rooms that share air can target one band over the day instead of fighting. Until now schedules were stored strictly per `(zone, profile)`, so two open-door bedrooms with "the same" plan were independent copies that drifted — one heating while the other cooled. Now you create a first-class named schedule (`comfort_band.create_shared_schedule`, e.g. *Bedrooms*) and assign rooms to it with a new per-zone `select.{zone}_schedule_assignment` (options `Own schedule` + every shared name; default `Own schedule`). An assigned zone resolves its bands from the shared schedule live — edit it once (`comfort_band.set_schedule` with `shared_id` instead of `zone`, or the card's schedule editor) and **every** member room follows in the same refresh. Shared schedules stay profile-aware (home/away still resolve separately), feed MPC lookahead/pre-heat and band-ramp smoothing identically to own schedules, and a per-zone override still wins over the shared band. It's an **additive overlay, not a migration**: each zone keeps its own `schedules` untouched and reverts to them losslessly the moment you switch back to `Own schedule`, a dangling assignment falls back to own→manual rather than erroring, and there's no behaviour change until you assign a room (`STORAGE_VERSION` unchanged). Management services: `create_shared_schedule` (optionally seeded by deep-copying a zone's own schedules), `rename_shared_schedule` (a pure name update — assignments are keyed by a stable id, so renames never detach a room), `delete_shared_schedule` (refuses while rooms are assigned unless `cascade: true`, which unassigns them first), and `assign_schedule`. The assignment select also exposes `schedule_id` + a `shared_schedules` summary (id / name / member rooms) as attributes so the companion card can render membership without extra round-trips. The websocket `get_schedule` / `subscribe_schedule` commands accept a `shared_id` too, and a shared edit fans out to every card subscribed to that schedule. The card UI for this lands in a follow-up release; the integration contract is back-compatible, so the current card keeps working unchanged.

**v0.13.0** adds opt-in, per-zone **deterministic fan-boost**. Comfort Band has always commanded `hvac_mode` + setpoint but never `fan_mode`, so a mini-split sat at whatever fan the user/unit last set. Now, when the applied action is heat/cool it commands a configured **active** fan (faster recovery), and when it drops to idle (`fan_only`) it commands a quieter **idle** fan — fixing the reported gym annoyance where a manually-raised fan stayed at full speed during fan-only air-cycling. A `switch.{zone}_fan_control_enabled` (default OFF) gates it; `select.{zone}_active_fan_mode` / `_idle_fan_mode` source their options live from the climate's `fan_modes` and store the chosen **string** (robust to a unit re-ordering its modes). Skip-on-`None` is intentional and asymmetric — set just `idle_fan_mode` to quiet a unit while air-cycling and leave the active side untouched. The command is guarded: skipped when the mode isn't in the unit's current `fan_modes` (unavailable / fanless / stale value), or already equals the current `fan_mode` (no redundant calls to cloud/mesh-routed units), and a unit that rejects `set_fan_mode` mid-transition is logged, not fatal. It only ever changes the `fan_mode` attribute, which the manual-edit detector ignores (v0.10.1), so it can never flush the learning buffer. One shared "active" fan covers heat + cool; default OFF means no behaviour change for existing zones.

**v0.12.0** persists the **idle slope** so MPC stays ready through a heating chase. `binary_sensor.{zone}_mpc_ready` needs an `idle_slope` *and* a recovery slope present in the same 90-min sample window — but a heating-dominated room (the gym chasing a rising morning band) produces only 1–3 min idle blips (below `SLOPE_MIN_SAMPLES`), and any sustained overnight idle ages out of the window before the morning rise. So `idle_slope` went `None` exactly when pre-heat was needed and MPC silently fell back to the reactive predictor. The idle (passive heat-loss) rate is a *slow-changing* property, so the integration now remembers the last good one beyond the window: when the live estimate is `None`, a recent persisted value is substituted (via `dataclasses.replace`, tagged `method_idle="cached"`) so readiness survives the chase. Persisted to storage (survives restarts), expired after 24 h, and cleared on every buffer flush (manual edit / sensor swap) so a stale model can't mislead MPC. `sensor.{zone}_thermal_slope` gains `idle_slope_source` (`live` / `cached` / `none`) and `idle_slope_cached_age_min`. Only the idle slope is persisted — recovery slopes change faster and are always present during a chase. The cache is scoped to MPC: `mpc.is_ready` / `mpc.plan` and the thermal-slope diagnostics consume it, but the reactive hysteresis and the v0.7 predictor always run on the **live** slopes — so a zone not running MPC sees no control change, and a cached idle can never silently suppress a reactive heat/cool call.

**v0.10.1** fixes a learning-buffer flush bug that kept MPC dormant on HVACs that vary their own fan speed. The manual-edit detector (`_on_climate_state_change`) compared `fan_mode`, so an HVAC modulating its fan autonomously — or reporting a different `fan_mode` in `fan_only` (idle) vs `heat`/`cool` — was misread as a manual edit and **flushed the entire sample buffer on every idle↔active transition**. The buffer therefore never held idle *and* recovery samples simultaneously, so `idle_slope` stayed `None`, `binary_sensor.{zone}_mpc_ready` never turned on, and MPC silently fell back to the reactive v0.7 predictor (no schedule-lookahead pre-heat). The detector now compares **only `hvac_mode` + `target_temp`** — still catching genuine manual setpoint/mode edits and physical-remote edits — while `fan_mode` continues to be captured per-sample (for future `(action, fan_mode)` slope partitioning). After upgrading, the buffer accumulates across fan changes; `mpc_ready` flips on once enough idle + recovery samples land, and anticipatory pre-heat engages.

**v0.10.0** adds **band-ramp smoothing** so schedule transitions can ease in instead of stepping instantly. Set `number.{zone}_band_ramp_minutes` to a non-zero value (e.g. 30) and the (low, high) band edges interpolate linearly within ±ramp/2 of each transition: a 4 °C overnight setback rising at 06:00 sharp becomes a 30-min shoulder from 05:45-06:15 instead of a wall. HVAC has time to ease into the new setpoint rather than chasing a sudden deficit. MPC's schedule lookahead (`upcoming_bands`) honours the same smoothing so its cost function sees the ramp shape too. Per-zone, opt-in: default 0 keeps the v0.9.x stepped behaviour for existing users. Range 0-120 minutes. Implemented in `schedule.resolve` / `schedule.upcoming_bands` with an optional `ramp_minutes` kwarg — predictor, hysteresis, and MPC consume the smoothed bands naturally via `effective_low` / `effective_high`. No new entities or behaviour changes for zones that don't opt in.

Pairs with the v0.9.1 diagnostics: if `sensor.{zone}_thermal_slope` shows `std_dev_idle ≈ 0` across many samples, the room sensor is masking drift — this OptionsFlow lets you swap to a finer sensor (e.g. a dedicated 0.1 °C sensor instead of the climate entity's built-in 0.5 °C reading) in one click.

**v0.9.1** adds per-segment diagnostics to `sensor.{zone}_thermal_slope` so users can spot when a slope estimate is based on resolution-limited data. New attributes: `sample_count_idle / recovery_heat / recovery_cool` (samples behind each per-action slope, vs the existing aggregate), `std_dev_idle / recovery_heat / recovery_cool` (standard deviation of sample temperatures in each segment, °C), and `method_idle / recovery_heat / recovery_cool` (always `"wls"` or `"none"` in v0.9.1, reserved string for future fallback methods). The signature failure mode this surfaces: a 0.5 °C-resolution sensor on a slowly drifting room can read the same quantized value for hours, producing many samples with `std_dev ≈ 0` — the WLS slope reports 0 not because the room is stable but because the sensor can't see the drift. MPC will defer to the reactive predictor + hysteresis in that regime. A wider fix (extending sample retention beyond 90 min so the slope estimator sees crossings outside the WLS window) is deferred to v1.0 — investigation showed the narrower fallback approaches don't help the realistic "no crossings in 90 min" case. **No behaviour change for zones with well-resolved sensors** — diagnostics-only release.

**v0.9.0** teaches the v0.8 MPC to look ahead over the active profile's schedule. Each refresh, the coordinator computes the per-minute `(low, high)` band the schedule resolves to over `mpc_horizon_minutes`, and `mpc.plan` feeds that into the cost function so each candidate action is scored against the band that will be active at end-of-horizon — not the band frozen at the snapshot. A heat-only zone with the morning band rising from `(16, 19)` to `(20, 22)` at 07:00 now starts heating *before* the rise instead of catching up after, closing the user-reported "morning pre-heat didn't happen" issue. Same release adds an **idle-preference tie-break** in the cost function: when `idle` achieves full-horizon in-band coverage (within one simulation step), it wins over heat / cool even if a recovery action lands closer to band midpoint. Closes the symmetric "cool fired in winter when the room would have settled passively" report — MPC stops burning compressor cycles on margin gains the room already has. Default `mpc_horizon_minutes` bumped 20 → 60 to give the lookahead enough runway for pre-heat ramps (existing zones keep their explicit value via presence-keyed backfill — only new zones pick up the new default). No new entities, no new user-facing knobs. **Behaviour change for existing v0.8.1 users with `mpc_enabled = ON`:** idle-preference fires whenever both idle and a recovery action achieve full-horizon coverage, so steady-state operation will see noticeably fewer heat/cool cycles than v0.8.1 (which favoured the recovery action via midpoint tie-break). Existing zones also keep their 20-min horizon — the schedule lookahead is technically active but only sees 20 min ahead, so pre-heat ramps for schedule transitions fire later than they would with the new 60-min default. Bumping `number.{zone}_mpc_horizon_minutes` toward 60 unlocks the full pre-heat window.

**v0.8.1.** Relaxes the v0.8.0 MPC cold-start gate so heat-only and cool-only zones activate MPC after the first idle + matching-recovery segment accumulates (was: required all three slopes). Per-refresh safety bail-out when the room genuinely needs the missing direction. No behaviour change for fully-equipped zones.

**v0.8.0** adds a **model-predictive controller** as a third decision layer in the stack. Each refresh, MPC enumerates a small action space (`idle`, `heat → band's high edge`, `cool → band's low edge`), simulates each forward over `mpc_horizon_minutes` (default 20 min) using the v0.6 thermal slopes, and picks the action that maximises projected time-in-band. Where v0.7's predictor anticipates *individual decisions*, MPC scores *whole cycles* — so it can pick `idle` even when the predictor would fire heat, or fire `heat` early when the predictor would wait. Behind a new opt-in `switch.{zone}_mpc_enabled` (default OFF) layered on top of `learning_enabled`. New diagnostic surfaces: `sensor.{zone}_mpc_action` (always populated for shadow comparison), `binary_sensor.{zone}_mpc_ready` (True once enough data has accumulated — typically a few hours of normal operation), and `number.{zone}_mpc_horizon_minutes` (range 10-60). The v0.7 predictor and v0.5 min-cycle gates still apply on top of MPC. Samples now also record the climate's `fan_mode` attribute (recorded but unused in v0.8 — v0.9 will partition slopes by `(action, fan_mode)` so MPC's action space can grow to include fan-mode candidates and close [#17](https://github.com/dpretty/ha-comfort-band/issues/17)). `climate.set_temperature` calls are now rounded to the climate entity's `target_temp_step` attribute (fallback 0.5 °C) so what we ask for matches what the HVAC actually pursues.

**v0.7.1.** Docs and test-isolation polish on top of v0.7.0; no behaviour change.

**v0.7.0** adds **passive drift acceptance** to the v0.6 predictive controller. When the room has crossed the deadband but the predictor's slope says we'll naturally return to band within `lookahead_minutes`, the predictor now suppresses the heat / cool call hysteresis would otherwise issue — letting the room recover on its own. Bounded by a per-zone comfort floor (`number.{zone}_passive_tolerance`, default 0.5 °C, set to 0 to disable): we'll never tolerate drift further than that from the band. Reuses the existing `learning_enabled` gate; default behaviour is unchanged for users who haven't opted into predictive control. **Behaviour change for existing v0.6 users with `learning_enabled = ON`:** the 0.5 °C default tolerance silently enables passive acceptance on next load; set `number.{zone}_passive_tolerance` to `0` to restore v0.6's "always defer to hysteresis on band exits" behaviour.

**v0.6.0** added **predictive control via a learned thermal slope** ([#11](https://github.com/dpretty/ha-comfort-band/issues/11)). Per-zone rolling-window slope estimator (segmented by HVAC action) projects room temperature forward by `lookahead_minutes` (default 5) and triggers heat/cool earlier when an idle drift will cross the deadband, or releases earlier when a recovery rate will overshoot the band. Three new entities per zone: `sensor.{zone}_thermal_slope` (°C/h with per-action slopes in attributes), `sensor.{zone}_predicted_action` (always populated for shadow-comparison against `current_action`), `number.{zone}_lookahead_minutes` (range 2-15). Gated by the existing `switch.{zone}_learning_enabled` — default OFF, so behaviour is unchanged unless you flip the switch. v0.5 cross-mode gate still applies on top of predictor decisions. A manual edit to the climate entity (outside our path) flushes the sample buffer so the slope estimator stays honest.

**v0.5.0** added **cross-mode min-cycle dwell** to suppress rapid `heat ↔ cool` mode flips ([#16](https://github.com/dpretty/ha-comfort-band/issues/16)). New per-zone `number.{zone}_cross_mode_min_minutes` defaults to the zone's current `min_cycle_minutes` (8 by default) — `heat → cool` and `cool → heat` transitions now wait that many minutes after the last action. Idle releases (`heat → idle`, `cool → idle`) still fire immediately so a heat or cool cycle can always stop. **Behaviour change for existing users:** mode flips previously fired instantly; set the new entity to `0` to restore that.

**v0.4.0** added apparent-temperature support: a per-zone optional **humidity sensor** (configurable via ConfigFlow or a new OptionsFlow on existing zones), a new `sensor.{zone}_apparent_temperature` (Steadman 1994 simplified; equals room temp when no humidity is configured), and a per-zone `switch.{zone}_use_apparent_temperature` toggle that feeds the apparent value into hysteresis decisions instead of the raw room reading. Decisions fall back to the raw reading automatically when the humidity sensor is unavailable. Also adds `switch.{zone}_learning_enabled` — a master gate for the v0.4+ learning cluster (#9, #11); no decision effect at the v0.4.0 release (v0.6.0 activated it for predictive control).

**v0.3.0** added full profile CRUD: four new services (`create_profile`, `clone_profile`, `rename_profile`, `delete_profile`) and a new `SIGNAL_PROFILE_LIST_CHANGED` dispatcher signal so the singleton select entity (and any subscribed cards) re-render on every profile mutation. The `default_profile` is now tracked per-store and survives renames — whatever profile holds that role cannot be deleted. Built-in profiles narrowed from `home / away / sleep` to **`home` + `away`**; existing installs keep any `sleep` profile they had as a normal user profile that can now be renamed or deleted.

**v0.2.0** added a `comfort_band/subscribe_schedule` websocket command so the card receives live schedule updates from any source without polling.

Every zone still defaults to **shadow mode** (`switch.{zone}_enabled = off`): the integration computes the heat/cool/idle decision and updates the `current_action` sensor, but does **not** call `climate.set_hvac_mode`. Flip the switch on per zone when you're ready to cut over.

## Setup walkthrough

1. **Add the profile manager** (one-time): Add Integration → Comfort Band → *Set up profile manager*. This creates `select.comfort_band_profiles_active_profile` with the built-in profiles `home` + `away`. Use the card's Profiles tab (or the `create_profile` / `clone_profile` services) to add your own.
2. **Add a zone**: Add Integration → Comfort Band → *Add a zone*. You'll be asked for:
   - **Zone name (slug)**: lowercase letters / digits / underscores, e.g. `office`. Becomes part of every entity_id.
   - **Climate entity**: the `climate.*` entity Comfort Band will eventually drive (e.g. a Mitsubishi mini-split).
   - **Room-temperature sensor**: a `sensor.*` of `device_class: temperature` to watch.
   - **Humidity sensor (optional)**: a `sensor.*` of `device_class: humidity`. When set, enables apparent temperature ("feels like"). Can be added / changed / cleared later via **Configure** on the zone entry.
   - **Fallback (v0.18.0, via Configure only)**: an optional second temperature sensor to read from while the main one is unavailable, and whether the climate entity's own reading may be used as the last resort (on by default). Either kicks in after the main sensor has been dark for five minutes.
3. Repeat for every room.

## Predictive control (v0.6, extended in v0.7)

Each refresh, the integration appends a `(timestamp, decision_room, action)` sample to a per-zone rolling buffer (90 min, rate-limited to one sample per 60 s, persisted across restarts). It then computes three slopes via weighted least-squares regression with exponential recency weights (`τ = 20 min`), each over the most recent run of its action — consecutive samples with that label and, since v0.20.0, no gap of more than 17 minutes between consecutive ones, so a slope is not fitted across an outage of the room sensor, the climate entity or Home Assistant itself that lasted longer than that:

- **`idle_slope`** — passive drift while idle (the room cooling overnight, warming in the sun). Fitted from the idle run less any samples in the first 30 minutes of the idle stretch it belongs to (v0.19.0), which belong to the cycle before it rather than to the room — measured from where the stretch began, across any gap (v0.20.0).
- **`recovery_slope_heat`** — how fast the room rises while heat is running.
- **`recovery_slope_cool`** — how fast the room falls while cool is running.

MPC (below) plans with the same three, with two stand-ins the reactive controls never see. One is a cached idle slope (v0.12.0). The other is a recovery slope for a cycle too young to have its own, carried over from the previous cycle of that action (v0.21.0).

When `switch.{zone}_learning_enabled` is ON, the predictor projects `decision_room + slope × lookahead_minutes` forward (where `decision_room` is the apparent temperature when `use_apparent_temperature` is ON, otherwise the raw room reading) and:

- **Anticipates startup**: while idle, if the projection crosses the deadband edge (`low - deadband_below` or `high + deadband_above`), it triggers heat / cool *now* instead of waiting.
- **Anticipates shutoff**: while heating or cooling, if the projection overshoots the band edge (`high` or `low`), it releases to idle *now* so the room peaks at the edge instead of past it.
- **Passive drift acceptance** (v0.7): when the room has already crossed the deadband but the slope says it'll return to band within the lookahead window, the predictor stays idle and lets the room recover on its own (rather than firing heat / cool that would interrupt the natural drift). Bounded by `number.{zone}_passive_tolerance` (default 0.5 °C; 0 disables) — we'll never tolerate drift further than that from the band edge, even if the slope says recovery is "in progress". A second jitter guard requires the forecast to move the room by at least 0.1 °C toward the band, so a sensor-noise slope can't spuriously suppress a real heat call.
  - Concrete example: with `low = 20 °C` and the defaults (`passive_tolerance = 0.5`, `deadband_below = 0.3`), heat is suppressed only when the room is at or above 19.5 °C and below 19.7 °C (`low - deadband_below`) AND the slope is positive enough that projection lands inside the band; below 19.5 °C hysteresis always fires.
  - Caveat: the slope reflects the most recent ~90 min of samples. A sharp change in heat load (cold snap, opened window) won't show in the slope until the buffer catches up; passive acceptance may briefly suppress a legitimate heat call until the comfort floor is hit. The 0.5 °C default bounds the worst-case dip.

`sensor.{zone}_predicted_action` is populated *regardless* of `learning_enabled`, so you can shadow-compare against `sensor.{zone}_current_action` before flipping the switch on. Tune `number.{zone}_lookahead_minutes` (default 5, range 2-15): higher values trigger heat/cool sooner — useful for slow-responding systems (underfloor heating, large rooms) where temperature reacts to commands several minutes after the fact. Lower values are appropriate for fast-responding mini-splits, or if you find the predictor over-eager.

Existing gates still apply on top: predictor-anticipated heat is still subject to `min_cycle_minutes`, and predictor-anticipated heat↔cool flips are still subject to `cross_mode_min_minutes`.

**Known limitation:** if you manually change `hvac_mode` or `target_temp` on the climate entity (outside Comfort Band), the integration logs an INFO message and flushes the sample buffer — the slope estimator needs ~90 min of contiguous data to re-converge. Setting it back via a Comfort Band number entity is fine; the buffer is only flushed on external state changes. Nor, while a slow unit is still catching up, is setting it to exactly what Comfort Band just commanded — that is a change it would have made itself, so there is no stale dynamic to flush. While it is still catching up the two are compared field by field, so a change that lands on Comfort Band's value for one and the unit's own for the other is accepted too. Once the unit has caught up, or once one manual edit has been detected, every subsequent change is compared against what the unit was last seen reporting — which, for a unit that publishes no target in its current mode, keeps the last target it did report rather than nothing. As of v0.17.1 a setpoint the unit stops reporting altogether is not an edit either — many units drop it on reaching `fan_only` — though any target that vanishes while the mode stays where Comfort Band expects it is accepted along with it — moving a dual-mode unit from a single target to a temperature range is one route there rather than the only one. A unit that *permanently* coerces the setpoint — snapping it to its own whole degree, say — never catches up by that definition, so for those the field-by-field acceptance stands until the next command. (`fan_mode` is **not** in this list as of v0.10.1: many HVACs vary their own fan speed autonomously or report a different fan_mode in `fan_only` vs `heat`/`cool`, so comparing fan_mode flushed the buffer on every idle↔active transition and starved MPC of the idle+recovery sample mix it needs. fan_mode is still captured per-sample for future `(action, fan_mode)` slope partitioning.)

## Model-predictive control (v0.8)

Where the v0.7 predictor anticipates *individual decisions*, v0.8's MPC scores *whole cycles*. Each refresh, with both `learning_enabled` and `mpc_enabled` ON and `mpc_ready` True:

1. **Enumerate** a small action space — currently `{idle, heat → inputs.high, cool → inputs.low}`. (v0.9 will grow this to include per-fan-mode candidates.)
2. **Simulate** each candidate forward over `mpc_horizon_minutes` (default 60 min as of v0.9.0; existing zones keep their explicit value from earlier versions) by integrating at 1-minute steps using the matching learned slope (`idle`, `recovery_heat`, or `recovery_cool`). From v0.9.0, the band scored against at each step reflects the active profile's schedule at that minute — so a step crossing a band transition is judged against the band edges that will be active AT that step, not frozen at refresh time.
3. **Score** by minutes-in-band over the horizon, with a midpoint-distance tie-break to stabilise indeterminate cases.
4. **Pick** the highest-scoring action.

Why heat targets the band's **high** edge (not `low`): the target_temp value goes into `climate.set_temperature`. Aiming the climate at `low` lets its own internal hysteresis release heat as soon as the room reaches `low`, leaving the room oscillating across the band edge (the original v0.7 gym observation). Aiming higher means the climate keeps heating until *we* issue idle. The MPC's cost function re-evaluates idle every refresh and picks it once projected drift-down stays inside band longer than projected continued heating. Net effect: longer heat cycles, fewer compressor starts, and (usually) room oscillation that stays inside the band.

**Cold-start gate (v0.8.1+).** MPC needs `idle_slope` (the cost-function baseline) and at least one recovery slope (`recovery_heat` or `recovery_cool`) to compare candidates. Heat-only zones get MPC the moment idle + heat slopes accumulate; cool-only zones once idle + cool are available; fully-equipped zones pick from all three candidates. Until then `binary_sensor.{zone}_mpc_ready` is `off` and the coordinator silently falls back to the v0.7 predictor. The `mpc_action` sensor still populates during the warm-up (it mirrors the predictor's decision while not ready) so you can flip `mpc_enabled` on whenever you're ready — MPC takes over the moment the gate flips. Since v0.21.0 a cycle too young for a recovery slope of its own plans with the previous cycle's, the one MPC started it with. So MPC stays ready through the first samples of a cycle that follows one long enough to fit, and from then on the cycle's own slope decides. At these zones' five-minute cadence that lets MPC run the cycles it starts. Since v0.22.0 a running cycle's own fit also waits until its run has been watched for ten minutes, so at about a sample a minute a unit slow to move the room no longer fits the wrong way round at the fourth sample and loses the cycle to the reactive path. Before v0.21.0 MPC lost the slope on the refresh after each start, and the reactive path released the cycle. A previous cycle too short to fit lends nothing; since v0.22.0 MPC does not end a cycle while it is planning it on a borrowed slope, unless the room reaches the band edge the cycle drives it towards, so a cycle MPC starts leaves a slope to lend unless that edge, or another of the exits listed there, ends it first (see v0.22.0 under Status). Nothing is carried into a cycle that resumes after a gap in sampling, once its first sample after the gap is in, and the previous cycle must still be in the sample buffer, which is pruned to 90 minutes as samples arrive.

**Safety bail-out (v0.8.1+).** If the room is clearly outside band on a side whose recovery slope hasn't accumulated (e.g. a heat-only gym suddenly needs cooling), MPC defers to the predictor for that refresh — the predictor's hysteresis fires the correct direction reactively. `mpc_ready` stays `on` (MPC is still equipped to act in normal scenarios); the bail-out is observable by `mpc_action` mirroring `predicted_action` during the brief out-of-band episode.

**Recommended rollout.** Enable `learning_enabled` first; watch `predicted_action` track `current_action` for a few days. Then enable `mpc_enabled` and compare `mpc_action` against `predicted_action` (visible in the device's diagnostic section). The two should agree most of the time but periodically diverge — MPC stays idle longer, fires heat earlier, cools to a different edge. Once the divergence pattern looks right, leave `mpc_enabled` on and tune `mpc_horizon_minutes` (longer horizon = more conservative).

**Energy trade-off.** Targeting the band's opposite edge means heat / cool cycles run longer than in v0.7 — fewer cycles but more energy per cycle. v0.9's fan-mode extension will add a fan-speed penalty to the cost function so MPC can prefer "heat at fan_low for longer" over "heat at fan_high for shorter" when both score similar time-in-band.

Existing gates still apply on top: MPC-anticipated heat is still subject to `min_cycle_minutes`, and MPC-anticipated heat↔cool flips are still subject to `cross_mode_min_minutes`. Setpoint commands are rounded to the climate entity's `target_temp_step` (fallback 0.5 °C) before being issued so our `_last_command_state` snapshot matches what the climate will actually settle at.

### Profile manager entity attributes

`select.comfort_band_profiles_active_profile` carries two extra attributes the card (or any consumer) can read alongside the standard `options` / `state`:

- `default_profile` — the rename-aware fallback target. Initially `"home"`; tracks renames. The profile pointed to by this attribute cannot be deleted.
- `descriptions` — a `{profile_name: description}` map; each profile's optional description as set via `create_profile` / `clone_profile`.

The card uses presence of `default_profile` as a feature flag — if it's missing, the card knows it's talking to a pre-v0.3.0 install and hides the CRUD affordances.

## Importing a legacy schedule

If you're migrating from a hand-rolled YAML system that stored each zone's schedule as 48 `input_number` helpers (one each for low and high, per hour 00..23), the `comfort_band.import_legacy` service will collapse them into a transition list and seed the zone's **default profile** (initially `home`; tracks renames).

In **Developer Tools → Services**:

```yaml
service: comfort_band.import_legacy
data:
  zone: office          # Comfort Band zone slug
  source_zone_name: office  # slug used by your legacy input_number entities
```

Adjacent identical hours collapse into a single transition, so a flat 24-hour band yields one transition at `00:00`. The service raises if any helper is missing, unavailable, or non-numeric.

## Other services

Schedule mutations: `set_schedule`, `add_transition`, `update_transition`, `remove_transition` — each targets **either** a zone's own schedule (`zone:`) **or** a shared schedule (`shared_id:`); supply exactly one. Per-zone override control: `start_override` / `cancel_override`. Comfort feedback (v0.11.0): `record_feedback` (logs `too_hot` / `just_right` / `too_cold` for the v3 learning loop; read back via the `comfort_band/get_feedback` websocket command). Profile management: `set_profile` (switch active), `create_profile`, `clone_profile`, `rename_profile`, `delete_profile`. Shared schedules (v0.14.0): `create_shared_schedule` (optional `seed_from_zone` deep-copies that zone's own schedules), `rename_shared_schedule`, `delete_shared_schedule` (refuses while rooms are assigned unless `cascade: true`), `assign_schedule` (point a zone at a shared schedule, or back to its own with `shared_id` omitted). All zone-scoped services take the bare zone slug, not an entity_id. See `services.yaml` or **Developer Tools → Services** for the full schemas.
